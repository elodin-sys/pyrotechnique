From the perspective of the International Space Station (ISS), an orbital "day" lasts only 90 minutes, meaning astronauts witness 16 day-night cycles every 24 hours. [1] 
As the ISS passes through different parts of this rapid cycle, the atmospheric glow changes dramatically in color, layering, and visibility: [1, 2] 
## ☀️ Orbit Day (The Sunlit Side)

* 
* What you see: A brilliant, deep blue band hugging the curve of the Earth.
* The Science: The atmosphere is experiencing dayglow. While dayglow is actually the brightest form of airglow, it is largely overwhelmed and washed out by the extreme glare of the sun. The intense blue you see is simply sunlight scattering through the dense lower layers of air (Rayleigh scattering). [1, 3, 4, 5, 6] 
* 

## 🌅 Orbital Sunset & Sunrise (The Twilight Limbs)

* 
* What you see: An outline of fire—vibrant layers of deep orange, fiery red, and subtle purple.
* The Science: As the sun slips behind or emerges from the edge (limb) of the planet, sunlight bends and stretches through thousands of miles of dust, clouds, and air. The shorter blue wavelengths are scattered away, leaving only the long-wavelength reds and oranges to cut through. [1, 7, 8] 
* 

## 🌑 Orbit Night (The Dark Side)

* 
* What you see: A striking, ghostly neon green ribbon floating above the dark planet, often topped by a faint orange or muted red layer.
* The Science: This is nightglow. Free oxygen atoms created by daytime UV rays slowly recombine under the cover of darkness, releasing a constant green light. Simultaneously, an orange-red band appears slightly lower down, caused by excited hydroxyl (OH) molecules and sodium from vaporized space dust. [3, 4, 9, 10, 11, 12] 
* 

## 🌌 Bonus: Extreme Solar Activity (The Auroras)
If the ISS passes near the polar regions during an active solar storm, the calm bands of airglow are completely eclipsed by curtains of brilliant green, deep purple, and neon red. These are auroras, which are far more dynamic, rapidly shifting, and vibrant than the steady, subtle bands of daily airglow. [10, 13, 14]
